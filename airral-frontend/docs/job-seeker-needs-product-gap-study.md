# AIRRAL Job-Seeker Needs and Product Gap Study

Last updated: 2026-08-14

## Purpose

This study answers three questions:

1. What are job seekers actually struggling with?
2. Which of those problems does AIRRAL solve today?
3. What should AIRRAL build next to become a better product rather than another job board?

The recommended product position is:

> AIRRAL helps a job seeker find real, relevant jobs, decide which ones deserve their time, submit a truthful stronger application, and keep the search moving until they get interviews.

AIRRAL should optimize for good applications and interview progress, not listing volume, scrolling, or social engagement.

## Research Method

The study combines:

- Candidate surveys from iHire, Employ, LinkedIn, Gartner, Criteria, Monster, Jobscan, and TopResume.
- ATS funnel data from Ashby.
- Job-scam guidance from the U.S. Federal Trade Commission.
- Qualitative job-seeker discussions from Reddit.
- A code and data audit of the current AIRRAL applicant portal.

Survey results are directional rather than perfectly comparable because samples, regions, question wording, and sponsor incentives differ. Reddit is used only as qualitative evidence, not prevalence data.

## What Job Seekers Are Missing

### 1. Confidence That a Job Is Real and Still Open

Trust is now a first-order job-search problem.

- iHire's 2025 survey found ghosting was the most cited challenge at 59.0%, followed by fake, fraudulent, or ghost jobs at 39.3%.
- Gartner found only half of candidates trusted that jobs they were applying for were legitimate.
- The FTC warns that scammers advertise through job sites and social media and may seek money or identity information.
- Job seekers repeatedly describe stale, reposted, or apparently fake jobs as wasted effort.

Sources:

- https://www.ihiretechnology.com/resourcecenter/employer/pages/the-state-of-online-recruiting-2025
- https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them
- https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams
- https://www.reddit.com/r/recruitinghell/comments/1mf9ozb/what_is_the_hardest_part_of_the_job_search_for/

What users need:

- Official-source attribution.
- Confirmation that the apply link still works.
- First seen, last verified, and employer-posted dates shown separately.
- Repost and duplicate detection.
- A visible explanation of why AIRRAL believes the role is active.
- A report-stale or report-suspicious action.

### 2. A Fast Answer to "Is This Worth Applying To?"

Users want decision clarity before spending time.

iHire found candidates wanted:

- Hiring timeline transparency: 60.5%.
- Salary range: 57.1%.
- Hiring-manager or recruiter contact: 40.7%.
- Must-have and nice-to-have qualifications separated: 36.2%.
- Detailed responsibilities: 35.7%.
- Work mode clarity: 31.9%.
- Shorter applications: 31.2%.

Monster's 2026 U.S. survey found 60% would not apply without a salary range. Negative reviews, unclear descriptions, unrealistic requirements, and long processes were also important deterrents.

Sources:

- https://www.ihiretechnology.com/resourcecenter/employer/pages/the-state-of-online-recruiting-2025
- https://www.monster.com/career-advice/research/job-search-dealbreakers
- https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/what-candidates-want-in-2025

What users need:

- A concise apply, review, or skip recommendation.
- Evidence behind that recommendation.
- Salary and compensation confidence.
- Required versus preferred qualifications.
- Role, seniority, location, work-mode, visa, and experience conflicts.
- Estimated application effort and likely interview burden when known.
- Company stability and reputation context.

### 3. Relevant Jobs Instead of Search Noise

Job seekers struggle to find jobs that actually suit them.

- Jobscan found 30% of respondents struggled to find jobs matching their skills and qualifications.
- iHire found 27.5% struggled to find jobs they were qualified for, while 25.8% struggled with being overqualified.
- Remote work, salary, location, benefits, and visa sponsorship are not secondary preferences; they can be hard constraints.

Sources:

- https://www.jobscan.co/state-of-the-job-search
- https://www.ihiretechnology.com/resourcecenter/employer/pages/the-state-of-online-recruiting-2025

What users need:

- Hard constraints separated from ranking preferences.
- Role-family and career-track matching, not loose keyword overlap.
- Explanations for why each role appears.
- Controls for must-have skills, avoid terms, salary floor, relocation, work authorization, and source trust.
- Honest adjacent-role suggestions for career changers.

### 4. Fast, Truthful Resume Tailoring

Candidates believe tailoring matters but cannot afford to rewrite every application manually.

- Jobscan found 26% said tailoring each resume takes too much time; ATS compatibility and balancing brevity with completeness were major related problems.
- TopResume found only 28.5% tailored every application, while 29.7% tailored most applications.
- Gartner found 39% used AI in applications, but only 26% trusted AI to evaluate them fairly.
- Job-seeker discussions consistently describe tailoring as exhausting and uncertain.

Sources:

- https://www.jobscan.co/state-of-the-job-search
- https://topresume.com/career-advice/jobseeker-trends-report
- https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them
- https://www.reddit.com/r/recruitinghell/comments/1mf9ozb/what_is_the_hardest_part_of_the_job_search_for/

What users need:

- A reliable resume parse they can review and correct.
- Required, core, and preferred gap analysis.
- Evidence-backed bullet suggestions using only real candidate experience.
- A small set of high-impact edits, not generic advice.
- A version of the resume attached to each application.
- Clear disclosure of uncertainty and no encouragement to fabricate skills.

### 5. Control After Clicking Apply

The application often becomes a black box.

- Employ found 71% expected an application to take under 30 minutes and 35% would abandon one that took too long.
- Re-entering resume information, forced talent-network registration, and account creation were specific abandonment triggers.
- Communication, prompt feedback, easy applications, and flexible scheduling were major positive experience factors.
- iHire's top challenge was applying and never hearing back.

Sources:

- https://pages.lever.co/rs/659-JST-226/images/2025-Job-Seeker-Nation-Report.pdf
- https://www.ihiretechnology.com/resourcecenter/employer/pages/the-state-of-online-recruiting-2025

What users need:

- Application status, date, resume version, contact, notes, and next action.
- Follow-up reminders that create a useful message draft.
- Expected response windows based on the user's own history or clearly labeled aggregate data.
- A stale application prompt, archive action, and emotional closure.
- Conversion feedback: applications to screens, screens to interviews, interviews to offers.

### 6. Company and Interview Intelligence

Candidates increasingly evaluate employer risk, not only role fit.

- Criteria found 67% investigated company financial health before accepting a role.
- LinkedIn found compensation, work-life balance, and flexible work remained the top broad priorities, with meaningful variation by occupation.
- TopResume found candidates researched company websites, job descriptions, and employer-review sites before interviews.

Sources:

- https://www.criteriacorp.com/blog/candidate-experience-report-findings
- https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/what-candidates-want-in-2025
- https://topresume.com/career-advice/jobseeker-trends-report

What users need:

- Hiring, layoff, funding, leadership, and product-change signals attached to a company or job.
- Clear source dates and confidence.
- Role-specific interview themes and questions grounded in the posting.
- A preparation workspace using the candidate's actual resume evidence.

### 7. Better Paths Than Anonymous Inbound Applications

Inbound applications dominate volume but have weak conversion.

Ashby analyzed more than 38 million applications across 93,000 jobs. Referrals were a small share of applications, but 40% of referred candidates progressed from application to interview compared with 3% of inbound candidates in its dataset.

Source:

- https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals

What users need:

- A low-pressure referral and outreach plan for high-priority jobs.
- A place to record known contacts and outreach status.
- Useful outreach drafts grounded in a real connection or shared context.
- No attempt to turn AIRRAL into a social feed.

## Current AIRRAL Inventory

Local database snapshot on 2026-08-14:

- 5,200 active jobs.
- 129 active sources.
- 127 companies with active sources.
- 100 companies represented in the active posting cache.

Current product strengths:

- Jobs is the default applicant destination.
- Desktop split view and mobile detail drill-in.
- Cached jobs from direct ATS and career sources.
- Source, freshness, work mode, salary, quality, seniority, and visa fields.
- Personalized profile match and selected-job resume fit.
- Resume parser v3 with cross-industry skills, experience estimation, confidence, and warnings.
- Required, core, and preferred fit analysis with truthful rewrite guidance.
- Saved jobs and persisted tracker status.
- Follow-up and job-alert notification foundations.
- Company and market signal services exist in the backend.

## Recommended Product

The product should be organized around four connected decisions.

### 1. Discover

Show relevant, active jobs that satisfy hard constraints.

Required capabilities:

- Role and skill relevance.
- Location and work-mode constraints.
- Salary floor and salary-listed preference.
- Work authorization constraints.
- Source and freshness trust.
- Career-change mode for adjacent roles.

### 2. Decide

For every selected job, produce one evidence-backed decision object:

- `APPLY`, `REVIEW`, or `SKIP`.
- Decision confidence.
- Reasons to apply.
- Blocking conflicts.
- Missing information.
- Required and preferred qualifications.
- Salary, company, source, freshness, visa, and application-effort facts.
- Next best action.

This decision must come from the backend so list cards, selected detail, saved jobs, alerts, and mobile all use the same truth.

### 3. Prepare

Turn resume fit into a controlled application-preparation flow:

1. Review parsed resume facts.
2. Confirm or correct role, dates, skills, and evidence.
3. Select a job.
4. See required, core, preferred, experience, and role gaps.
5. Choose truthful evidence for the most important gaps.
6. Generate or edit a small set of bullet improvements.
7. Save a job-specific resume version.
8. Complete the apply checklist.

### 4. Progress

Make the tracker operational:

- Quick status change.
- Applied date and source.
- Resume version.
- Contact and outreach state.
- Next action and due date.
- Follow-up draft.
- Interview date and preparation notes.
- Outcome and rejection reason when known.
- Search funnel metrics and prompts based on conversion, not activity volume.

## What Not To Build Now

- A generic social feed.
- Rooms or messaging as primary navigation.
- One-click mass apply.
- An opaque AI score with no evidence.
- Generic resume rewriting detached from a selected job.
- Employer reviews without a credible data source and moderation model.
- Unlabeled salary, timeline, or stability guesses.

Mass apply would amplify the exact problems users report: low-quality inbound volume, more tailoring anxiety, weaker trust, and more ghosting.

## Prioritized Delivery Plan

### P0: Trust and Decision Foundation

1. Fix source-sync cleanup so complete successful source scans deactivate jobs no longer present.
2. Add repost and duplicate detection using normalized company, title, location, description hash, and source history.
3. Replace vague source badges with `Official ATS`, `Company career page`, `Last verified`, and confidence explanations.
4. Move apply/review/skip logic to a backend decision response.
5. Show required, preferred, experience, role, location, salary, and visa conflicts in selected detail.
6. Fix filtered pagination and applied-filter visibility.
7. Replace `Visa-friendly` with explicit sponsorship states.

### P0: Resume Preparation

1. Add a parsed-resume review and correction screen after upload.
2. Show parser confidence and warnings.
3. Let users confirm which skills have real experience evidence.
4. Display full missing-requirement categories from the fit analyzer.
5. Add evidence-safe bullet editing and job-specific resume versions.

### P0: Operational Tracker

1. Add status controls to each card.
2. Add next step, due date, notes, and applied date editing.
3. Surface overdue follow-ups.
4. Link each tracked job to its fit result and resume version.
5. Add filters and sorting for next action, status, and date.

### P1: Company and Interview Context

1. Attach relevant hiring, layoff, funding, leadership, and product signals to selected jobs.
2. Clearly label source, event date, and uncertainty.
3. Generate a job-specific interview brief from the posting and confirmed resume evidence.
4. Add interview schedule, preparation notes, questions, and follow-up draft.

### P2: Referral and Learning Paths

1. Add private contact tracking for high-priority jobs.
2. Generate context-aware outreach drafts without pretending a relationship exists.
3. Turn genuine missing skills into small learning plans with free resources.
4. Suggest adjacent roles when the target market is too narrow.

## Success Metrics

North-star metric:

> Qualified applications submitted per active job seeker using a verified active source and a reviewed resume.

Supporting metrics:

- Resume upload to first useful job decision.
- Selected job to resume-fit completion.
- Resume-fit completion to apply.
- Saved to applied conversion.
- Follow-up completed by due date.
- Application to recruiter screen.
- Screen to interview.
- Interview to offer.
- Stale or invalid job report rate.
- Percentage of job decisions with sufficient source confidence.
- User corrections to parsed resume fields.
- Time spent preparing each qualified application.

Guardrail metrics:

- False `active` or `official` labels.
- False sponsorship-positive labels.
- Resume suggestions rejected as inaccurate.
- Applications encouraged despite hard-constraint conflicts.
- Notification opt-outs.

## Immediate Recommendation

Build the next slice in this order:

1. Trustworthy active-job verification and source cleanup.
2. One backend `Job Decision` contract used everywhere.
3. Resume review plus truthful job-specific tailoring.
4. A tracker that drives next actions and follow-ups.

This sequence compounds. Better job truth improves decisions; better decisions make tailoring worth doing; tailored applications make the tracker meaningful; tracker outcomes then improve future recommendations.
